EDER POETRY / HIGH SCHOOL · BETRAYAL AND SILENCE · ABAB

The Boy Who Learned to Doubt

14th March 2023 · 21:17 EAT

I was still wearing a school shirt when I learned that trust can bend,
A friendship can look solid until it reaches its end.
We shared the same corridor, the same jokes, the same small plans,
Then one careless story changed the way I understood your hands.

You spoke to me like family when nobody else was near,
So I never thought your kindness could be carrying fear.
A secret left my mouth and somehow found another door,
By morning half the school knew what I had told you before.

I walked through crowded corridors pretending I was fine,
Laughing at the right moments, keeping everything in line.
But every familiar face became a question in disguise,
And every friendly greeting felt like another pair of eyes.

That afternoon the rain came hard against the classroom pane,
I watched it draw crooked rivers like handwriting made of pain.
I wanted to blame the weather, the school, the world, the day,
But betrayal has a quiet way of making trust decay.

I did not become colder in a single afternoon,
It happened slowly, like a shadow crossing over noon.
I stopped explaining everything, stopped giving every truth,
Because some lessons arrive too early, even when you are youth.

Years later I can name the wound without becoming its prisoner,
The boy I was still deserves a little kindness from the listener.
He trusted because he had not learned the cost of being known,
And losing one safe person made the whole world feel unknown.

So if I guard my heart today, it is not because I hate,
I simply learned that open doors still need a careful gate.
I kept the lesson, left the bitterness where it belonged,
And carried forward quietly, still believing I could be strong.

EDER POETRY / HIGH SCHOOL · FIRST LOVE AND LOSS · AABB

October Left the Lights On

28th October 2023 · 22:43 EAT

We were young enough to call forever something simple,
A word that sounded certain on a cracked school window.
You smiled and I believed the future knew your name,
I thought two people wanting love could keep the feeling the same.

We traded little promises between lessons and the bell,
Small messages that made an ordinary afternoon feel well.
I carried your name home like a song I could not lose,
And even boring evenings seemed to offer better news.

Then something changed before either of us could explain,
Your replies grew shorter, then disappeared again.
I searched through every sentence for a reason I could hold,
But love can leave a room before the story has been told.

October kept arriving with the same familiar rain,
The same old streets were standing, but nothing felt the same.
I passed the places where we used to stop and talk,
And heard your absence following quietly beside my walk.

I wanted one more conversation, one honest final night,
Not to force the door open, only understand the flight.
But silence has no signature, no place to sign or blame,
It leaves you holding questions while it carries off a name.

I hated that I missed you when I knew I had to grow,
I hated that a broken thing could still feel warm below.
But losing you taught me that love is not possession,
And sometimes letting go is the hardest form of affection.

So October left its lights on long after you were gone,
And I learned that lonely evenings still eventually become dawn.
I do not call that ending proof that love was ever wrong,
Some people are a chapter, and some chapters make you strong.

EDER POETRY / HIGH SCHOOL · ACADEMIC DRAWBACKS · ABAB

Marks on the Report Card

9th February 2024 · 06:31 EAT

The paper came back folded in the middle of the page,
A number sat there quietly, but somehow felt like rage.
I knew I had worked, yet the mark refused to agree,
And suddenly one red correction felt larger than me.

I watched other names climb while mine remained behind,
Wondering if intelligence was something you could find.
The classroom had a rhythm, but I kept missing the beat,
And every unfinished question made defeat feel complete.

At home I learned to hide the paper underneath a book,
Because disappointment has a language in a parent's look.
Nobody had to shout for me to understand the weight,
Sometimes a quiet room can make a failure feel like fate.

I started counting weaknesses as though they were my name,
Turning every poor result into evidence of shame.
One setback became another, then another joined the line,

There were mornings I arrived tired, carrying more than school,
Trying to solve equations while fighting an invisible rule.
The lesson continued whether my mind was clear or not,
And I learned that being present does not mean you have the strength you've got.

Still, a poor mark never measured every hour I gave,
It only captured one small moment on a page.
I began to study failure like a subject of its own,
Looking for the places where my habits could be grown.

I still remember those numbers, but they no longer own the room,
They are footprints from a season, not a prophecy of doom.
The student who once feared a grade could define his whole life,
Began to understand that progress survives a temporary strife.

EDER POETRY / GIVING UP · DEPRESSION AND HOPE · ABBA

A Heart Can Be Tired Too

17th May 2024 · 01:52 EAT

There was a night when every plan looked distant and unreal,
When getting out of bed required more strength than I could feel.
The ceiling held my thoughts like stains that would not disappear,
And tomorrow sounded less like promise, more like fear.

I had become good at saying, “I'm okay, I will manage,”
While carrying private storms behind an ordinary passage.
People saw the work, the jokes, the clothes, the steady face,
But nobody saw how hard it was to keep pretending there was grace.

Giving up did not arrive like thunder in the sky,
It sounded more like silence asking why I even try.
I began to measure every dream against the weight of now,
And wondered whether I had reached the limit of my vow.

Then morning came without fixing anything at all,
No miracle appeared to catch me when I felt I might fall.
But sunlight touched the curtain and stayed there for a while,
And somehow that ordinary light managed to interrupt the trial.

I learned that hope can be embarrassingly small,
Sometimes it is answering one message, sometimes taking one call.
Sometimes it is drinking water, opening a book, or leaving the room,
Tiny acts that tell the darkness it cannot decide the bloom.

I did not become fearless; I simply stopped demanding proof,
I let one surviving thought become a temporary roof.
The future did not promise me an easy road to walk,
But it gave me enough morning to keep moving block by block.

If I ever say I am tired, let that sentence be allowed,
A heart can need a pause without becoming weak or bowed.
I have learned that surviving does not always look heroic or bright,
Sometimes it is just choosing to remain until another light.

EDER POETRY / ESCAPE AND CONSEQUENCE · PERCocets · AABB

The Silence I Borrowed

26th July 2024 · 00:47 EAT

There was a season when the noise inside became too loud,
When I could stand among a room and still feel outside the crowd.
I wanted one quiet hour where the memories would cease,
So I chased a borrowed silence and mistook numbness for peace.

I remember thinking Percocets could make the distance disappear,
Not understanding how quickly escape can turn into fear.
The point was never glory, never some reckless display,
I was trying to outrun feelings I did not know how to say.

For a little while the edges seemed softer than before,
Then morning brought the same old weight waiting at the door.
Nothing had been solved, nothing had truly changed,
I had only moved the pain somewhere darker and rearranged.

The dangerous part was how ordinary the escape could feel,
How a temporary quiet can pretend that it is a cure for real.
But numbness is not healing and silence is not peace,
And anything that asks you to disappear is charging too high a fee.

I began to understand the difference between relief and repair,
Between hiding from a wound and letting somebody know it is there.
I wanted to be strong, but strength had started looking strange,
So I learned that asking for help can also be a form of change.

I do not romanticize that chapter or call the darkness deep,
I remember what it cost me when I wanted only sleep.
The lesson is not the substance, the lesson is what I missed,
A person can need compassion long before they need to be fixed.

Now when the old escape whispers that it knows my name,
I remember that surviving is not the same as feeding pain.
I choose another doorway, even when the doorway feels small,
Because a difficult morning is still a morning, and I would rather see it all.

Now if I love again, I will not love with blinded eyes,
I will keep my own direction while I walk beneath shared skies.

The kind of heart I carry forward, or the love I someday choose.

The semester slipped away, but I remained inside the story,
Not with a perfect record, but with reasons to keep growing.
A setback can leave a mark without becoming the final line,
Sometimes the comeback begins the moment you stop calling failure your identity.

What stayed after you left was not only sorrow or regret,
It was the person I became through everything we met.

But I can choose what those memories become inside my head,
A prison made of yesterday, or soil where something new is bred.

I do not promise that tomorrow will be painless or clear,
I only promise I will meet it instead of disappearing from here.
If I need help, I will say so; if I need rest, I will rest,
Because asking for another hand is not a failure of the quest.

I have dreams that still look bigger than the person I can see,
But perhaps that is exactly why they keep calling out to me.
There is work to finish, pages waiting, roads I have not known,
And a future that will never arrive if I refuse to leave my home.

So today I kept the door open, even if only by a thread,
Letting in enough light to challenge everything the darkness said.
I am not finished, not defeated, not the sum of what went wrong,
I am still here, still becoming, still learning how to carry on.
